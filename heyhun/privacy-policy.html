Hey Hun Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 August 2026

Hey Hun is made by LiveStack Studios. This policy explains what the app collects, why, and what you can do about it. It describes what the app actually does — if we change that, we change this page.

Hey Hun records intimate information: your cycle, your moods, your symptoms, what you need on a hard day. Some of it can be sent to a person you choose. This page is specific about which parts, because you should not have to guess.

The short version. Everything you log is private to your account by default. Nothing reaches another person until you create an invitation and someone accepts it. Even then, they receive a filtered summary built on our server — never your raw log. Your private notes and the sensitive parts of your Care Profile can never be shared, because we did not build a way to share them. You can stop sharing instantly, at any time, without asking anyone.

What we do not collect

  • Apple Health data. Hey Hun has no HealthKit integration at all. It does not read from or write to Apple Health.
  • Your location. We never request it. Our analytics provider estimates an approximate region from your IP address.
  • Your contacts, photos, or camera. The app asks for none of these.
  • Voice recordings. See Dictation — speech is transcribed entirely on your device and no audio is ever recorded, uploaded, or stored.
  • An advertising identifier. There are no ads in Hey Hun and no ad networks in the app. We never show Apple's tracking prompt because we have nothing to track you with.
  • Payment details. The app is free during the beta. We never see card details.

What a supporter can see

A supporter is a person you invite — a partner, father, mother, or friend — who uses our companion app, Men's True Aide. They see nothing until you generate an invitation code and they redeem it.

Once connected, they never read your logs. Our server builds a separate summary once a day and puts only that summary where their app can reach it. Their app has no permission to open your logs, your cycle history, your private notes, or your Care Profile.

What is on by default when you create an invitation

A new connection starts with these turned on, and you can turn any of them off at any moment:

  • Your current cycle phase and day
  • Your moods
  • Your symptoms
  • Your energy level
  • Your predicted next period date

These start off, and only turn on if you switch them on:

  • Your shareable note — the free-text note, sent word for word if you enable it
  • Your Care Profile summary (see below)

Alongside this, a supporter always receives a daily suggested action and a short "do this / not this" line. These are generated from the information you have chosen to share. If you have shared nothing, they are generic.

Not shared in this version, at all. Your cravings and wishes, and your daily context signals (sleep, pain, stress, social battery, appetite) are not sent to a supporter in the current release. The settings exist in our data model but there is no way to switch them on, so they remain off for everyone. If a future version makes them available, it will be as a choice you make, and we will update this page first.

What can never be shared

These are not settings you have to remember to keep off. There is no mechanism to share them:

  • Your private notes. They are stored in a separate area that no sharing code path can read, and no part of our server ever opens them.
  • Your allergies, dietary limits, medications, and period products. These are used on our server to filter out unsuitable suggestions before a supporter ever sees them. The values themselves are never sent.
  • The notes attached to your cycle records. Only the start date is ever read.
  • Mood intensity, flow intensity, and your stress tags. You can log them; they never leave your account.
  • Your date of birth, your email address, and your notification tokens.

Your Care Profile

The Care Profile is optional and starts fully off. If you turn on Care Profile sharing for a connection, here is exactly what happens — this is more detail than most policies give, because "a summary" is too vague for something this personal:

  • Treats you love, your comfort menu, and little wins are sent in your own words, limited to five items each and shortened to 60 characters. They may also appear inside a suggested action, so a supporter can see the item you wrote.
  • How to support you is converted into fixed phrases we wrote. Your own wording is not sent.
  • Things to please never do are sent in your own words, but unattributed — they are mixed into a general "not today" list along with generic guidance, sorted alphabetically so their order gives away nothing. A supporter sees the guidance; they are not shown a labelled list of your objections.
  • Essentials — allergies, dietary limits, medications, period products — are never sent, as described above.

Stopping

You can revoke a connection at any time, from Settings or from the connection itself, with no confirmation from the other person and no notice to them beyond the sharing stopping. Revoking takes effect immediately: their app loses access at once, and our server deletes every daily summary and every suggested action for that connection.

Two things survive a revocation: the connection record itself, which we keep so the same invitation cannot be replayed, and the supporter's own activity record — their streak and score, which contain no information about you beyond the fact that a connection existed. Both are removed if you delete your account.

Notifications

We ask permission only at a moment when it is useful, never at first launch, and the app works fully if you decline. We send: a note when someone redeems your invitation, a note when they finish setting up, and — only if you opt in — a weekly recap. The weekly recap contains a day count and a single word for your cycle phase. It never contains anything you logged.

Dictation

Hey Hun can transcribe speech into a note. Transcription is forced to run on your device; audio is never sent to Apple, never sent to us, and never written to storage. The resulting text appears in an editable field, and nothing is saved until you save it. If you decline microphone or speech permission, the microphone button disappears and the app does not ask again.

Analytics

We use two things, and neither one records what you log.

  • Anonymous daily counters. Our own server keeps a per-day count of how many times certain things happened across all users — for example, how many cravings were logged today. These are plain integers. They carry no account identifier, no date of birth, no text, and nothing that could point back to you.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase, limited to nine milestone events: completing signup, starting and completing the Care Profile intake, completing a pairing, adding or switching a target, entering demo mode, saving a sharing toggle, and revoking a connection. We attach no parameters and no user ID to any of them. Notably, the sharing-toggle event does not record which setting you changed or what you changed it to — that would reveal your sharing posture, so it is not collected. Google's SDK does collect its own standard information, including an app-instance identifier, device and operating system details, and an approximate region derived from your IP address.

Who we share with

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with data brokers or advertisers. There are no advertisements in Hey Hun. We use these providers, who process data on our behalf:

  • Google Firebase — sign-in, database, server functions, notifications, analytics. Firebase privacy
  • Apple — Sign in with Apple, and delivery of notifications.
  • Google — Google Sign-In, if you choose it.

Data is stored on Google Cloud infrastructure in the United States. We may disclose information if we are legally required to, and we will tell you unless we are prohibited from doing so.

How long we keep things

Your logs, cycle history, and Care Profile are kept until you delete them or delete your account — we do not expire them, because a cycle app is only useful with history. Daily summaries and suggested actions for a connection are deleted the moment that connection is revoked. Invitation codes expire after 14 days.

Deleting your account

Settings → Delete Account, with a typed confirmation. This removes your profile, every daily log, your cycle history, your private notes, your Care Profile, and every connection you are part of along with its summaries, suggested actions, and activity records. It cannot be undone.

We retain the invitation-code records associated with your account. They contain identifiers and timestamps, no health information and no content. Anonymous daily counters are aggregate totals and cannot be separated out. Our server's operational logs may retain account identifiers for a period set by our hosting provider.

If you cannot get into the app, email JA@livestackstudios.com from your account's address and we will do it for you.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete the personal information we hold, and to object to some processing. Email JA@livestackstudios.com and we will respond within 30 days. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

Age

Hey Hun is for adults 18 and over. We ask your date of birth at sign-up and do not let you continue if you are under 18. The app is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a minor has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Security

Access is enforced on our server, not merely hidden in the app: our database rules restrict every document to the account that owns it, and a supporter's access is re-checked against your current sharing settings on every single read. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest by our hosting provider. No system is perfect, and we will notify affected users and the relevant authority if a breach occurs.

Changes

If we change this policy we will update the date at the top, and we will announce changes that affect what can be shared inside the app before they take effect.
